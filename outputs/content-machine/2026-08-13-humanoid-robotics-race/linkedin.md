# LinkedIn Post: The Humanoid Robotics Race

Three companies are racing to build humanoid robots. Only one of them is profitable.

Figure AI just raised at a $39B valuation, the best-funded humanoid robotics company on the planet. Real deployment too: 90,000+ parts loaded in an 11-month BMW pilot. Still pre-revenue.

Tesla has 1,000+ Optimus robots physically on the Fremont factory floor right now. Sounds like proof. Except Elon Musk said it himself on the January earnings call: those units are "primarily for learning, not productive tasks."

Then there's Unitree. No mega-raise. No headline valuation until their IPO priced this month at $9B. They shipped 5,500+ humanoid robots in 2025. They're profitable, the only one of the three that is.

I see the same pattern every time I sit down with a business owner evaluating an AI vendor. The most impressive pitch deck isn't the signal. The size of the funding round isn't the signal. What's actually running, and actually working, is the signal.

mAxIm's whole approach is built around that: real automations, real hours saved, nothing staged for a demo day. If a vendor can't show you something live and working today, the valuation doesn't matter.

Which one are you actually buying: the promise, or the thing that works?
