# Newsletter: The Humanoid Robotics Race

I've been watching the humanoid robotics race the last few weeks, and there's a pattern in it that has nothing to do with robots.

Three companies, three totally different bets.

Figure AI raised at a $39 billion valuation, the best-funded humanoid robotics company in the world. They've got a real deployment too: an 11-month pilot at BMW's Spartanburg plant, 90,000+ parts loaded. Genuinely impressive. Also genuinely pre-revenue.

Tesla's got over 1,000 Optimus robots on the Fremont factory floor right now. On paper that looks like the strongest proof of the three. Then Musk said it himself on the January earnings call: those units are "primarily for learning, not productive tasks."

And then there's Unitree. No mega-raise. No headline valuation, until their IPO priced this month at $9 billion, the first humanoid robotics company to actually go public. They shipped over 5,500 units in 2025. They're profitable. The only one of the three that is.

Here's the deep take: none of this tracks with funding size. The company throwing off the least hype is the one making money.

I see the same pattern constantly with clients evaluating AI vendors for their own business. The pitch deck isn't the signal. The size of the raise isn't the signal. Whether something is actually running today, for real, not staged for a demo, that's the only signal that matters.

That's the build philosophy behind mAxIm. Every automation I ship has to actually run before I show it to anyone. Not a mockup, not a roadmap slide. If it's not live and working, it doesn't go in the pitch.

So here's the question worth asking the next AI vendor who pitches you: what's live right now, not what's coming next quarter.
