# Unitree

Price-to-performance leader out of China, first humanoid robotics company to actually IPO. General market research requested by James, not a FirmaTRUST BD prospect.

## Background
Chinese robotics maker, originally known for quadruped robots, now leads with the G1 humanoid. Unlike nearly every other humanoid startup, Unitree is profitable.

## Signals
- IPO'd on the Shanghai exchange (priced Aug 2026) — first mainland-listed humanoid robot maker. Raised ~$904M at a ~$9B valuation; retail tranche was 8,000x oversubscribed.
- 2025 revenue rose 4x+ to ~$252M (1.7B yuan); humanoid sales (867.8M yuan) overtook quadrupeds as the largest business line for the first time.
- Shipped 5,500+ humanoids in 2025 with positive adjusted profit (~$83M / 600M yuan) — rare in this sector, where most competitors are burning cash pre-revenue.
- G1 priced at $13,500-$16,000; R1 (launched July 2025) at $5,900 — dramatically undercuts Figure and Tesla on price.
- Caveat: Q1 2026 adjusted profit fell 52.6% as R&D and marketing spend ramped, so profitability is compressing even as revenue grows.
- China overall: Chinese manufacturers captured ~90% of global humanoid shipment volume in 2025-2026 (est. ~18,000 units, up 508%), competing on cost and manufacturing speed rather than AI capability.

## Edge
Only major humanoid player that's actually profitable and IPO'd — proof of a real unit-economics model at $5,900-$16,000 price points, backed by China's manufacturing base and cost structure.

## Likelihood of winning
Strong odds of winning the volume/cost segment specifically — not the AI-capability segment. Unitree isn't trying to out-build Figure's Helix or Tesla's FSD stack; it's winning on shipped units and profitability today, not roadmap promises. IPO proceeds give it capital to defend price leadership as Chinese competitors (UBTECH, Agibot, EngineAI) crowd the same lane. Real risk: margin compression already showing in Q1 2026 as it spends to keep pace on capability.

## Suggested angle
Relevant comp for FirmaTRUST's own positioning: profitable, shipped-and-working beats well-funded-and-promised. Reinforces FirmaTRUST's "ship real automations, not roadmap slides" pitch.
